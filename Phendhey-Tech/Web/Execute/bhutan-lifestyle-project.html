I am exploring how lifestyle habits affect health and wellbeing. This helps examine how daily routines — sleep, work, and screen use — differ between urban and rural residents in Bhutan, and how these differences influence sleep quality and daytime energy. Using these insights, I aim to develop evidence-based tools, like a website and mobile app, to help individuals and communities improve their daily habits and overall wellbeing.

Who did we survey?

We collected 366 responses from people living in urban, rural, and semi-urban areas of Bhutan. After removing one response from a non-resident, 358 were used for analysis. Most people who responded were from cities — which reflects how Bhutan's population is shifting towards urban areas.

02

How do city and village life differ?

Urban people tend to work a bit longer and do more mental work (like office jobs). Rural people use screens a little more before bed and sleep slightly longer — but the sleep difference is so small it barely matters. The real difference is what kind of work people do, not how long they sleep.

What actually affects your sleep?

We looked at three habits to see if they help or hurt sleep quality and daily energy. Think of it simply: Red = bad for sleep, Grey = no real effect, Green = slightly good.

How each habit affects sleep & energy
Habit 🏙️ Urban 🌾 Rural

💡 Plain English: Putting your phone down before bed is the single most important thing you can do for better sleep — in both cities and villages.

06

Key Takeaways — The Big Picture

Here is what all five findings add up to, in plain language:

📱
Screen use is the biggest problem. It hurts sleep quality and daytime energy for everyone — city and village alike.
🏙️
City and village lifestyles are measurably different. Urban people work more; rural people use screens more at night. Both groups struggle with sleep — just for different reasons.
🛏️
Sleeping longer doesn't automatically fix things. How well you sleep matters more than how long.
📲
People want a simple phone app. No fancy gadgets — just something easy that fits into daily life.
✅
Over 95% are open to a wellness app. The demand is real. The main barriers are awareness and access — not interest.

What about the "Maybe" group?

The 36.6% who said "maybe" are not against the idea — they just haven't thought about it yet, or they're unsure of the cost. That's not rejection. That's an opportunity to educate and reach them.

What kind of tool do people want?

The most popular choice was a sleep tracking app — something simple that already lives on the phone they own. No extra devices. No complicated setup. Just a quiet tool that helps them sleep better.

💡
Top choice: A simple sleep tracking app on your existing smartphone — affordable, easy to use, no new hardware needed.

Bottom line

The demand is real. People know what they want. The main barriers are awareness (people don't know what's available) and access (internet and smartphones aren't equal everywhere in Bhutan). These are solvable — and that's exactly what this project is working towards.
